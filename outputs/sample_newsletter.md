# The Growing Adoption of AI Agents: Security Risks and Opportunities

AI agents are moving beyond experimentation and becoming part of how organizations work. They can assist employees, interact with tools, access information, and carry out tasks with increasing autonomy. But as adoption grows, so does an important question: are organizations prepared to manage the risks that come with these systems?

Research from the [Cloud Security Alliance](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/enterprise-ai-security-starts-with-ai-agents) highlights how employees are already using AI agents, including agents that may operate without formal organizational approval. This creates a challenge for businesses: employees may introduce AI tools into their workflows faster than security teams can establish oversight.

The gap is not limited to security. A [Deloitte survey](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/deloitte-survey-examines-ai-readiness-agentic-ai-success.html) examining enterprise AI readiness points to a difference between organizations' ambitions for AI adoption and their ability to realize its benefits. Having access to AI tools does not automatically mean existing processes and workflows are ready to support them. Successful adoption also requires changes to how people work alongside these systems.

The governance challenge becomes even more important when agents can take actions rather than simply provide recommendations. In [The Authorization Gap: Why Yesterday's Controls Won't Work with Today's Agents](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/authorization-gap-ai-agent-governance), BCG discusses questions around agent identity, permissions, and accountability.

For organizations, this means knowing what an agent can access, which actions it is authorized to perform, and who is responsible for its decisions. Audit trails and controlled permissions become important parts of managing these systems.

## Where the opportunity lies

These challenges also create an opportunity to rethink how organizations build and manage AI systems.

- **Founders** need to consider governance and security before deploying agents into critical operations.
- **Developers** need to design systems with controlled permissions, clear identities, and auditability.
- **Business operators** need to review workflows and determine where human oversight is necessary.

AI agents offer opportunities to improve how work gets done, but adoption alone is not a measure of success. Organizations need to build the processes and safeguards that allow these systems to operate responsibly.

**The real opportunity is not simply deploying more AI agents. It is making them useful, accountable, and secure.**
