# The Growing Adoption of AI Agents: Security Risks and Opportunities

AI agents are becoming an increasingly common part of the modern workplace. From assisting employees with everyday tasks to interacting with business systems, these tools are changing how organizations approach automation and productivity. However, as their adoption grows, so do concerns about security, authorization and responsible use.

The Cloud Security Alliance highlights the growing use of AI agents across organizations. Its research shows that employees are using agents, including those that are not authorized. This raises an important concern for businesses: employees may be introducing AI tools into their workflows without adequate organizational oversight.

The challenge extends beyond controlling which tools employees use. Organizations must also consider what these agents can access and what actions they are permitted to perform.

A Deloitte survey examining enterprise AI readiness highlights a gap between organizations' ambitions for AI adoption and their preparedness to realize its benefits. While businesses recognize the potential of AI, existing processes and workflows are not always ready to support its effective implementation. The survey also emphasizes the importance of transforming workflows and developing new models of human-agent collaboration.

Security and governance are crucial as agents can now interact with systems and perform actions.

In The Authorization Gap: Why Yesterday's Controls Won't Work with Today's Agents, the discussion focuses on the need for stronger approaches to agent identity, authorization and accountability. A useful audit trail should allow organizations to understand what an agent was trying to accomplish, what information it accessed, which tools it used and who had the authority to intervene.

These challenges also create opportunities for organizations to rethink how they build and manage AI systems. Developers can design agents with clearly defined permissions, controlled access and audit trails. Businesses can establish governance policies and introduce human oversight where necessary. Founders and operators can evaluate how agentic systems fit into existing workflows without overlooking security responsibilities.

The growing adoption of AI agents presents both an opportunity to improve business operations and a responsibility to manage these systems carefully. Their success will depend not only on what they can accomplish, but also on whether organizations can deploy them securely and accountably.

As AI agents become more integrated into the workplace, the question is no longer how much work they can automate. It is how organizations can make that automation both useful and secure.
